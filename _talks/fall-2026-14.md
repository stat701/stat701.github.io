title: "Causal Inference with Text Outcomes: LLMs as Thermometers"
---

In many applications, researchers may aim to understand how some intervention changes writing, from students’ essays to social media posts. Standard approaches in causal inference score each observed text, such as with a letter grade or sentiment rating, and estimate treatment effects on those scores. However, human evaluation is costly and automated models may be difficult to develop or not generalize across settings. Moreover, we may be interested in how individuals would perform on tasks beyond their observed writing. We propose using large language models as “thermometers” for text outcomes. We adapt copies of a common model to treatment and control writing, then compare their performance on fixed external benchmarks. Our primary target is the average benchmark effect we would obtain with abundant writing from each person. When few texts per person are available, we compare pooled model training with training one model per individual. We bound estimation error in terms of within-person variability, between-person heterogeneity, and benchmark curvature. Simulations examine how performance depends on the learning procedure; an application to a randomized study of AI-assisted practice examines the approach on real writing.
