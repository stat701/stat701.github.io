title: "From Individual Covariates to Population Dynamics: Likelihood-Based Inference for Heterogeneous Epidemic Models"
---

Accounting for the wide heterogeneity of individuals remains a challenge in epidemic modeling. In this paper, we incorporate individual-level covariate information to improve inference on population-level epidemic parameters. We develop a closed-form likelihood via the Dynamical Survival Analysis (DSA) method, which can be used for both frequentist and Bayesian inference. Our method jointly estimates the infection rate, the recovery rate, and the covariate effects. It thereby identifies which covariates most strongly affect individual susceptibility to the disease and allows individuals to be ranked by their susceptibility. The method also accommodates under-reported case counts. We illustrate the approach using the SIR (Susceptible-Infected-Recovered) model with and without network structure; it can be extended similarly to the SEIR (Susceptible-Exposed-Infected-Recovered) model. We demonstrate our method on simulated data where the covariates are different biomarker levels.
