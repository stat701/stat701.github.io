title: "Adaptive Decision Boundaries for Bayesian Optimal Interval Dose-Finding Designs"
---

The Bayesian optimal interval (BOIN) design is widely used in early-phase trials for its prespecified dose-escalation rules. However, its interim decisions use only current-dose data and ignore information from other doses. We propose a cross-dose borrowing extension that preserves BOIN's three-state decision structure while allowing data-adaptive boundaries. At each eligible decision, a leave-one-dose-out dose--toxicity model predicts current-dose toxicity using other-dose data. These predictions provide cross-dose evidence for the underdosing, target and overdosing hypotheses, which is tempered by agreement with current-dose data. The tempered evidence updates prior probabilities of the three BOIN hypotheses while keeping their locations fixed. Underdosing evidence raises the escalation boundary, while overdosing evidence lowers the de-escalation boundary. We also provide a rule for crossing decision boundaries. Simulations across dose--toxicity scenarios and toxicity targets compare the proposed design with fixed BOIN and other adaptive interval designs. Results show that cross-dose borrowing improves dose targeting while preserving an explicit BOIN-based allocation rule.
